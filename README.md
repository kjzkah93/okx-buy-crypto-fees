## OKX fees explained without the usual fog

There are several different costs that users often group together as “the fee.” They are not the same.

### Buy and Convert quote

The direct Buy and Convert flows may present one quoted price rather than a separate trading commission. The spread and conversion rate are reflected in the amount of crypto you receive. If two routes produce noticeably different amounts, compare the final received amount rather than relying on a label such as “zero fee.”

### Spot trading fees

For order-book spot trading, OKX uses maker and taker fees.

- A **maker** order adds liquidity to the order book and is generally an order that rests before being filled.
- A **taker** order executes immediately against existing liquidity.
- A limit order is not automatically a maker order. If it matches immediately, it may be treated as a taker order.
- A market order is usually treated as a taker order.

OKX calculates the spot fee using the applicable fee rate and the amount filled. The fee can be deducted from the crypto purchased or from the proceeds of a sale.

### Deposit fees

Crypto deposits generally do not carry an OKX network fee for the recipient. You still need to use the correct network and deposit address. Sending an asset over an unsupported network can create a recovery problem, and deposits below the stated minimum may not be credited automatically.

### Withdrawal fees

Crypto withdrawals do carry a fee, and the amount can vary by asset and network. OKX says withdrawal fees may change with network conditions and operational requirements. Always check the withdrawal page for the current amount before confirming.

### Futures funding fees

Futures are a separate category. In addition to trading fees, perpetual futures can involve funding payments between traders. OKX states that funding fees are separate from trading fees and are settled between long and short positions rather than being treated as a standard order-book commission.

For a simple “OKX buy crypto” transaction, you generally do not need futures. Adding leverage to a first purchase is a quick way to turn a basic purchase into a risk-management exam.
